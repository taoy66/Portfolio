## Problem Statement

**Tools:** SolidWorks

Co-packaged copper interconnects route high-speed signals between the chip and system through a compact connector, so mechanical loads can transfer into the chip substrate and degrade contact stability and signal performance.

In this project, I designed compression hardware to apply a controlled, repeatable clamp force across the connector and substrate stack-up.
In addition I designed strain relief to reduce bending and pull loads at the cable/connector interface.

The goal was to protect the chip substrate by reducing mechanical stress on the connector interface, while maintaining stable signal performance under handling, vibration, and thermal expansion.

---


## Design Approach

![](/projects/CompressionHardware_images/Compression_Hardware_Assembly.png)

My design introduces two mechanical improvements to stabilize the stack-up and isolate the connector from external loads.

•Top-side compression button + springs: applies a uniform, repeatable preload across the connector interface. This keeps contact pressure consistent and prevents uneven clamp loading that can concentrate stress into the chip substrate.

•Bottom-side strain-relief seating plate + springs: adds compliance under the cable exit region to absorb bending, pull, and vibration loads. This reduces rigid mounting forces and limits moment transfer into the connector contacts.

Together, these features improve mechanical robustness and help maintain reliable high-speed signal integrity by keeping the contact interface stable during real-world handling and thermal expansion.
